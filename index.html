02 The one decision per room

Every module is wired the same way. What changes room to room is the mode it runs in, and that follows a single question: is the light fitting itself smart?

Edge Mode

Ordinary bulb · module switches the power

The light toggles on every flip of the rocker, whichever way the plate happens to be sitting. Existing two-way wiring works as-is. The wall switch keeps working whether or not the server is up.

Detach Relay Mode

Smart fitting · power stays on permanently

A Hue bulb or a Tuya fan must never lose mains or it drops off the network. The relay is detached, so the rocker becomes a button that Home Assistant listens to and passes on to the light. Four of the eight circuits are like this, and the wall switch in those rooms needs the server to do anything.

05 Before you close anything up

  • Never put neutral or earth on S1 or S2

    Sonoff call this out explicitly. Those terminals take the wall switch contacts and nothing else — anything else damages the module.

  • The module needs a neutral at the point it is fitted

    Most older UK switch boxes have live and switched live only, because the circuit is looped at the rose. If there is no blue in the back box, the module goes up at the rose instead.

  • Do not fit a touch switch to S1 / S2

    Touch switches power their own electronics from the lighting circuit. A signal pair cannot run one. Plain mechanical rockers only.

  • Check the back box depth

    A shallow 16 mm box will not take the module behind the plate. That room's module goes at the rose.

  • Radar, not a PIR

    A PIR only sees movement, so standing at a bench or sitting still on the sofa reads as an empty room and the light or fan drops out. Radar holds while you are still.

  • The cat flaps are not smart, and that is fine

    Both flaps are microchip-activated but standalone — no hub, no app, nothing to connect to. Only the flap itself is doing the work, keeping other cats out. Nothing can be fitted to a flap to tell which way a cat went either, because it swings both ways. So the sensors watch the room inside each flap instead: a cat appearing in the Garage or the Kitchen came in, and the room going quiet means she went out.

  • Keep the temperature sensors away from the radiators

    Far wall from both the radiator and the fan. The radiator valves do report a temperature, but they read it at the radiator, so the figure runs high and swings whenever the heating fires. That is fine for a valve and wrong for deciding whether a room is too warm.

The two sockets are switched, not permanent

A tablet left on charge at 100% swells its battery and pushes the screen out of the bezel within a year or two. The Living Room and Office sockets are fed through spare modules so the charger can be cut automatically at 80% and switched back on at 40%. Two of the four spares are used here, which is what they are for.

The bedroom tablet plugs into the bed's USB ports by hand, so it needs no socket and no module. Plain Fire HD 8, not the Plus, so there is no wireless charging on any of them — all three are cable.

Agree the exact height and position with Zero before chasing anything — each socket has to sit behind the tablet, hidden by it, not beside it.

The two fan temperatures are deliberately different. A single figure would have the fan chattering on and off all evening every time the reading wobbled across it; leaving a degree and a half between them stops that.

The Living Room lamp works the same way for the same reason. Going dark is the only thing that switches it on, and it turns off when the room empties rather than when the light level comes back up — otherwise the lamp would light the room, decide the room was light, and switch itself off again. It also gets one go per sitting, so if you turn it off by hand for a film it stays off until you get up.

The two bedrooms are deliberately off-only. A light that switches itself on when someone walks in is the sort of thing that gets a sensor unplugged in the first week.

Worst realistic case is the air con running while the printer fuses and the desktop is under load — call it 3 kW, around 13 A. Comfortably inside a 20 A radial, with 32 A giving room for whatever gets added later. Cable size, burial depth and the earthing arrangement are Tony's calculation, not a number off this page — they depend on the run length and how it goes in.

  • The air con is not an electrician's job

    R32 is a fluorinated refrigerant, so connecting a split system legally needs an F-Gas registered engineer, even on a pre-gassed easy-fit unit. What Tony provides is the supply, an isolator next to the indoor unit, and a route for the pipework and condensate drain. Book the F-Gas engineer separately.

  • Put a data duct in the same trench

    While the ground is open, lay a spare duct for network cable alongside the armoured supply, with proper separation. Everything in that building — the desktop, the tablet, the air con — is better on wire than on a wifi extender, and digging the garden twice is the expensive way to find that out.

12 Still to come

Four things are deliberately not specced yet. They are here so they do not get forgotten or worked out twice.

Item Waiting on What the answer changes
Boiler thermostat What boiler is actually in the house Whether it takes OpenTherm or is plain on/off. OpenTherm lets the boiler modulate down instead of cycling, which is worth having and needs a different thermostat.
Smart meter Meters fitted, and which supplier Real-time consumption needs a Hildebrand Glow device reading the meter locally over MQTT. The supplier's own data comes through the DCC and runs about half an hour behind.
Kitchen notice board Whether it runs on battery or mains An e-paper family board — a Waveshare panel on an ESP32 running ESPHome, or a ready-made one. Battery lasts months between charges because the screen only draws power when it changes. Mains is the only version that needs the electrician, and it needs a socket behind it. If that is wanted, say so before the walls are shut.
Room-level presence Nothing — a decision, not a fact A small ESP32 running ESPHome as a Bluetooth proxy in each room, working out which one your phone is nearest. That is what lets a wall tablet or the phone switch itself to the room you have walked into. A few pounds each, plugs into a USB socket, no wiring.

Two of these can catch Tony out later. The thermostat may want a wire from the boiler to wherever it goes, so if the boiler position is known while the floors are up, pull one in. And a mains-powered notice board needs a socket behind it in the kitchen. Everything else here is battery or plug-in and can wait.

Room-level presence is the flashiest of the three and the fiddliest to live with — it handles someone settling in a room well and someone walking down a hall badly, and phones differ a lot in how consistently they can be seen. Worth trying with two proxies before buying six.

13 Handover

1

Fit and test one module first, not all six. Confirm the light works from the wall before going any further.

2

Leave the modules on their factory setting. Edge and Detach are set in software, from Home Assistant, not on the wall.

3

Tuya fittings get paired in the Smart Life app first. They appear in Home Assistant on their own after that.

4

Everything else — joining each module to Zigbee2MQTT, setting the mode per room, binding the detached switches to the Tuya lights — is done from the server afterwards.
