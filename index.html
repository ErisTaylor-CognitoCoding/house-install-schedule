02 The one decision per room

Every module is wired the same way. What changes room to room is the mode it runs in, and that follows a single question: is the light fitting itself smart?

Edge Mode

Plain fitting · module switches the power

The light toggles on every flip of the rocker, whichever way the plate happens to be sitting. Existing two-way wiring works as-is. The wall switch keeps working whether or not the server is up.

Detach Relay Mode

Tuya fitting · power stays on permanently

A smart light must never lose mains or it drops off the network. The relay is detached, so the rocker becomes a button that Home Assistant listens to and passes on to the light.

05 Before you close anything up

  • Never put neutral or earth on S1 or S2

    Sonoff call this out explicitly. Those terminals take the wall switch contacts and nothing else — anything else damages the module.

  • The module needs a neutral at the point it is fitted

    Most older UK switch boxes have live and switched live only, because the circuit is looped at the rose. If there is no blue in the back box, the module goes up at the rose instead.

  • Do not fit a touch switch to S1 / S2

    Touch switches power their own electronics from the lighting circuit. A signal pair cannot run one. Plain mechanical rockers only.

  • Check the back box depth

    A shallow 16 mm box will not take the module behind the plate. That room's module goes at the rose.

  • Only the head comes off

    The valve body and pin stay in the pipework, so the system does not need draining. Unscrew the manual knob, screw the TRVZB on in its place.

  • Check every radiator before you start

    An older house is often a mix of valve bodies from different decades. Work out which adapter each one needs first, rather than finding out halfway round with the heating off.

07 Sensors

Five battery sensors, all Zigbee. Nothing to wire, nothing to leave space for — they go up after the electrics are finished and the decorating is done.

Room Sensor Doing what
GarageSNZB-06PLight on when the cat or anyone goes in, off ten minutes after it empties
LoungeSNZB-06PPresence, so the fan only runs when someone is in
LoungeSNZB-02PRoom temperature, so the fan only runs when it is warm
Main bedroomSNZB-06PPresence, same job as the lounge
Main bedroomSNZB-02PRoom temperature
06P5.8 GHz radar, ~4 m over 100°. Holds presence while someone is still.
02P±0.2 °C, one CR2477, about four years. No display.

  • Radar, not a PIR

    A PIR only sees movement, so standing at a bench or sitting still on the sofa reads as an empty room and the light or fan drops out. Radar holds while you are still.

  • Keep the temperature sensors away from the radiators

    Far wall from both the radiator and the fan. The radiator valves do report a temperature, but they read it at the radiator, so the figure runs high and swings whenever the heating fires. That is fine for a valve and wrong for deciding whether a room is too warm.

08 Wall tablets

Three Fire HD 8 tablets running the dashboard. Two of them need the electrician; the bedroom one does not.

Position Needs Fed through
LoungeSingle socket behind the tabletSpare ZBMINI R2
OfficeSingle socket behind the tabletSpare ZBMINI R2
Main bedroomNothing — charged from the bed's own USB ports—

The two sockets are switched, not permanent

A tablet left on charge at 100% swells its battery and pushes the screen out of the bezel within a year or two. The lounge and office sockets are fed through spare modules so the charger can be cut automatically at 80% and switched back on at 40%. Two of the four spares are used here, which is what they are for.

The bedroom tablet plugs into the bed's USB ports by hand, so it needs no socket and no module. Plain Fire HD 8, not the Plus, so there is no wireless charging on any of them — all three are cable.

Agree the exact height and position with Zero before chasing anything — each socket has to sit behind the tablet, hidden by it, not beside it.

The two fan temperatures are deliberately different. A single figure would have the fan chattering on and off all evening every time the reading wobbled across it; leaving a degree and a half between them stops that.

10 Handover

1

Fit and test one module first, not all six. Confirm the light works from the wall before going any further.

2

Leave the modules on their factory setting. Edge and Detach are set in software, from Home Assistant, not on the wall.

3

Tuya fittings get paired in the Smart Life app first. They appear in Home Assistant on their own after that.

4

Everything else — joining each module to Zigbee2MQTT, setting the mode per room, binding the detached switches to the Tuya lights — is done from the server afterwards.

11 What the house can already talk to

Three radios are live on the server, so a fitting bought later only has to speak one of them. Anything that names none of these is cloud-only and will not reach the house.

ZigbeeZigbee2MQTT on a Sonoff ZBDongle-P. Local. The eight light modules, six radiator valves and five sensors all join this.
TuyaAlready connected, currently running the cat litter trays. The two ceiling fans join it once fitted. Goes via Tuya's cloud.
MatterServer added 16 Aug 2026, running on the same box. Local, no account. Nothing paired to it yet.

Matter is the one to look for on any future fitting — it works like Tuya but without the round trip to a cloud, so it keeps working when the internet does not.
