Nothing on the battery list is outstanding; the cells are waiting on the sensors, not the other way round. Alkaline, not rechargeable — Sonoff specify 1.5 V LR6 and LR03, and NiMH cells at 1.2 V leave a valve on 3.6 V against its rated 4.5 V. They also read as flat from the day they go in, so every low-battery warning in the house becomes noise.

Brand matters for leaking, not for capacity. These cells sit in a device for one to two years, and a leak writes off a £40 valve. On capacity the brands are close enough to ignore. On leaking, the weight of user reports goes against Duracell — most reported leaks are Duracell, often still in date — while Kodak and GP come out clean. Buy Kodak or GP, and check the date code whatever the label says: expired alkalines leak regardless of brand, and cheap multipacks are often near the end of their shelf life.

02 The one decision per room

Every module is wired the same way. What changes is the mode it runs in, and that follows one question and one question only: would cutting its power break the thing on the end?

Edge Mode

No — it is a dumb thing

A plain bulb, or a fan with a mechanical cord. Nothing in it to break, so the module simply cuts its power. The light toggles on every flip of the rocker, whichever way the plate happens to be sitting, and existing two-way wiring works as-is. Works with the server down.

Detach Relay Mode

Yes — and it has a wall switch

A Hue bulb or a Tuya fan. Cut its power and it drops off the network, so the relay is detached and never opens. The rocker stops switching anything and becomes a button that Home Assistant listens to and passes on to the fitting. Five of the eight circuits. Needs the server.

Always On

Yes — and it has no wall switch

The Office heater. Smart, with its own thermostat, and nothing on the wall is meant to switch it. So it gets no module at all — a permanently live fused spur, and the temperature is set over Wi-Fi. Its frost protection works with the server down.

The switch feels identical in Edge and Detach — that is the point

In both, you press the rocker and the light changes. Nobody using the room can tell them apart, which is exactly why the two are easy to muddle. The whole difference is behind the wall: in Edge the module cuts the power; in Detach it never cuts the power and only reports that you pressed. The one day you would notice is a day the server is down — the Edge rooms still work by hand, the Detach ones do not. Nothing else about the wiring, the plates or the back boxes changes between them.

05 Before you close anything up

  • The home server lives in the Master Bedroom cupboard — it needs a socket and a data point

    The unused cupboard in the Master Bedroom is where the home server goes. A double socket inside the cupboard and a network point back to the router, both first fix. Everything on this page runs on that machine — the Zigbee coordinator, the automations, the dashboards — so it is not a wifi job and it is not an extension lead through a cupboard door.

    It runs cool and quiet: all solid state, no spinning drives, and the processor sits around 30 °C in normal use. What it will not take is a sealed box. Undercut the door or vent it, leave a hand's width clear at the back, and it looks after itself.

    The garage was the other candidate and was ruled out. Cold is not the problem — condensation is, and an unheated garage with an up-and-over door swings between the two every wet day.

  • Nine presence sensors need a plug, not a battery

    The SNZB-06P runs a 5.8 GHz radar continuously, which no coin cell would last a week on. Sonoff's specification is 5 V 1 A over USB-C. Nothing needs wiring for this — the sockets are already in and this is a plates job. What it does mean is that every presence sensor has to sit within its lead's reach of a socket, which decides where it can go rather than the other way round.

    Two things follow. There are twelve BSEED plates with USB built in and nine sensors, so every sensor can have a plate of its own with three spare — no rationing, no plug-in adapters, no leads on show. That was not true when only five plates were planned. The three spare plates cover the Voice PE speakers, which take the same 5 V USB-C supply.

    And the radar reaches about 4 m over 100°, so these do not have to go at ceiling height. Mid-wall or on a shelf near a socket works, and in the hall and on the landing that is likely the only sensible answer. Walk the nine positions with Zero before ordering anything else.

  • Never put neutral or earth on S1 or S2

    Sonoff call this out explicitly. Those terminals take the wall switch contacts and nothing else — anything else damages the module.

  • The module needs a neutral at the point it is fitted

    Most older UK switch boxes have live and switched live only, because the circuit is looped at the rose. If there is no blue in the back box, the module goes up at the rose instead.

  • Do not fit a touch switch to S1 / S2

    Touch switches power their own electronics from the lighting circuit. A signal pair cannot run one. Plain mechanical rockers only.

  • Check the back box depth

    A shallow 16 mm box will not take the module behind the plate. That room's module goes at the rose.

  • Radar, not a PIR

    A PIR only sees movement, so standing at a bench or sitting still on the sofa reads as an empty room and the light or fan drops out. Radar holds while you are still.

  • Contact sensor on the up-and-over, not a tilt sensor

    A tilt sensor sounds like the right part for a door that swings up, but the Zigbee ones are slow — people using the Aqara on garage doors report the angle taking seconds to report. An ordinary contact sensor mounted where the door meets the frame when closed gives open and shut straight away. You lose "opening" and "closing" as states, which is not what was asked for anyway.

  • The garage is unheated

    The two garage contact sensors will get through batteries faster than the indoor ones and want an occasional check for condensation. Normal, just not the same interval as the rest. The presence sensor in there is on mains, so it is only the coin cells to watch.

  • Two of the contact sensors are deliberately the dearer model

    The cheaper SNZB-04PR2 has a bug logged against it in Zigbee2MQTT: it may fail to send the first action after a few hours of inactivity. On a front or back door that opens all day it never shows. On something that sits shut for days it is exactly the opening you wanted to hear about. So the back gate and the garage up-and-over get the older SNZB-04P, which has no such fault, and the other four get the PR2. They report the same things and pair the same way — only the model name differs. If SONOFF fix the PR2 the distinction stops mattering; both take OTA updates.

  • The gate sensor only survives if the case is properly sealed

    An SNZB-04P has no IP rating of its own — outdoors it corrodes at the battery contacts within months. In a sealed weatherproof case it is fine, but the seal is doing all the work, so check it when the batteries are changed.

  • The cat flaps are not smart, and that is fine

    Both flaps are microchip-activated but standalone — no hub, no app, nothing to connect to. Only the flap itself is doing the work, keeping other cats out. Nothing fitted to a flap can tell you which way a cat went, because it swings both ways, so that part is done by the room sensors: a cat appearing in the Garage or the Kitchen came in, and the room going quiet means she went out.

    A vibration sensor on each flap is worth having anyway, for the one thing it does give you honestly — a time it was last used. Stick it high on the flap, within about 20 mm of the hinge. The flap pivots at the top, so weight near the hinge barely counts: 11.5 g up there adds under a tenth to the effort of pushing it open, where the same sensor on the bottom edge would add half again. Near the hinge it also travels less and so feels a weaker swing, but the flap slamming shut is a sharp knock felt right across it, and the sensitivity is adjustable if it ever misses one. It reports a tilt angle too, but ignore that — the same lag that rules tilt out on the garage door rules it out here, and a flap swings for under a second.

  • The washing machine goes quiet in the middle of a cycle

    Soaking and draining are silent, and some of those pauses run past ten minutes, so "it has stopped shaking" on its own would announce a finished wash halfway through. It only counts as done if the machine did a proper spin first and has then been still for ten minutes. Stick the sensor to the side panel, not the front — the door flexes and the top gets things put on it.

  • Only the probe goes in the fridge — never the sensor

    A fridge or freezer is a metal box, and a battery radio sitting inside it drops off the network. That is why these two are the probe version and not the same 02P used in the rooms: the body magnets to the outside of the door and only the stainless probe goes in, on its lead, run through the rubber door seal. The seal closes over a thin cable without leaking. An 02P would also read wrong — it stops at −10 °C, and a freezer sits well below that.

  • Keep the temperature sensors away from the radiators

    Far wall from both the radiator and the fan. The radiator valves do report a temperature, but they read it at the radiator, so the figure runs high and swings whenever the heating fires. That is fine for a valve and wrong for deciding whether a room is too warm.

The two fan temperatures are deliberately different. A single figure would have the fan chattering on and off all evening every time the reading wobbled across it; leaving a degree and a half between them stops that.

    The Neo is low profile and drops only 180 mm, so the blades end up at 2.02 m.   • The heater gets a fused spur, not a socket

    It comes with no plug and draws 10.4 A, so it wants a switched fused spur behind it at the mounting height, on its own way in the consumer unit.   • Air conditioning is parked, and do not fit a big one later

    The room is 8 ft by 6 ft, which is 4.46 m². That is below the minimum floor area printed in the manuals of most portable air conditioners — the MeacoCool range bars anything under 5.2 m², De'Longhi under 15 m² — because they run on flammable R290 refrigerant. Only 5,000 BTU units, rated for over 4 m², are permitted in here. Nothing bigger, at any price. Nothing needs doing on site for it either way.

  • Put a data duct in the same trench

    While the ground is open, lay a spare duct for network cable alongside the armoured supply, with proper separation. Everything in that building — the desktop, the tablet, the heater — is better on wire than on a wifi extender, and digging the garden twice is the expensive way to find that out.

Three ways in, all reaching the same assistant. Nothing here is an install job — it is already working and listed so the whole system is in one place.

Say it“Hey Nova” to the speaker in the Kitchen, Living Room or Master Bedroom
Hold itPress and hold the phone’s power button
Tap itThe Talk to Nova button on any dashboard
VoiceHis own, not an off-the-shelf one

The wake word is a model trained here rather than one of the three the speaker ships with, so the house answers to its own name. It recognises about nineteen times in twenty and does not trigger off the television.

12 Still to come

Four things are deliberately not specced yet. They are here so they do not get forgotten or worked out twice.

Item Waiting on What the answer changes
Boiler thermostat What boiler is actually in the house Whether it takes OpenTherm or is plain on/off. OpenTherm lets the boiler modulate down instead of cycling, which is worth having and needs a different thermostat.
Smart meter Meters fitted, and which supplier Real-time consumption needs a Hildebrand Glow device reading the meter locally over MQTT. The supplier's own data comes through the DCC and runs about half an hour behind.
Bathroom & kitchen extractors Seeing the two fans, which means being in the house Both sensors are already bought and both spare modules are already owned, so nothing else needs buying — this is a survey, not a purchase. Three things to establish on the day. Does the bathroom fan have a built-in overrun timer that runs on after the light, and is it fed from the lighting circuit's switched live rather than its own supply? Is the kitchen one a cooker hood or a wall extractor? A hood is the awkward case — cutting its power will not bring it back at a speed, so it needs a different approach. And where does the bathroom fan isolate, since the module has to sit outside the bathroom zones. The control itself is settled: each room compares its own dew point against the rest of the house, so it works in January as well as August.
Room-level presence Nothing — a decision, not a fact A small ESP32 running ESPHome as a Bluetooth proxy in each room, working out which one your phone is nearest. That is what lets a wall tablet or the phone switch itself to the room you have walked into. A few pounds each, plugs into a USB socket, no wiring.

One of these can catch Tony out later. The thermostat may want a wire from the boiler to wherever it goes, so if the boiler position is known while the floors are up, pull one in. Everything else here is battery or plug-in and can wait. The kitchen notice board has come off this list — it is bought, it is mains, and the socket for it is in section 08.

Room-level presence is the flashiest of the three and the fiddliest to live with — it handles someone settling in a room well and someone walking down a hall badly, and phones differ a lot in how consistently they can be seen. Worth trying with two proxies before buying six.

13 Handover

1

Fit and test one module first, not all six. Confirm the light works from the wall before going any further.

2

Leave the modules on their factory setting. Edge and Detach are set in software, from Home Assistant, not on the wall.

3

Tuya fittings get paired in the Smart Life app first. They appear in Home Assistant on their own after that.

4

Everything else — joining each module to Zigbee2MQTT, setting the mode per room, binding the detached switches to the Tuya lights — is done from the server afterwards.

14 What the house can already talk to

Three radios are live on the server, so a fitting bought later only has to speak one of them. Anything that names none of these is cloud-only and will not reach the house — with one exception already in use, below.

ZigbeeZigbee2MQTT on a Sonoff ZBDongle-P. Local. The eight light modules, six radiator valves, twenty-five sensors and the two Hue bulbs all join this.
TuyaAlready connected, currently running the cat litter trays. The three ceiling fans join it once fitted. Goes via Tuya's cloud.
MatterServer added 16 Aug 2026, running on the same box. Local, no account. Nothing paired to it yet.
RingNot a radio — an account. Already connected, and the Garage camera joins it. Cloud only, polled every 60 seconds, and recorded clips need a Ring subscription. It stops when the internet does.

Matter is the one to look for on any future fitting — it works like Tuya but without the round trip to a cloud, so it keeps working when the internet does not.
